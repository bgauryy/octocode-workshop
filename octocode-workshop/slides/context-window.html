Build the stack one click per message, bottom up. The model has no memory between calls: every turn, the whole stack is sent again. Click 1, the system prompt: the agent's prompt plus the MCP server's instructions, laid down first and always there. Click 2, the tools: every tool's name, description and schema. That block is a real measurement: Octocode's own benchmark probe found 16,807 tokens in the first request with its 13 MCP tools, against 5,029 with a single Bash tool. That 13 is the set the probe measured (Sep 2026), not the tool count on the what-is-Octocode slide: today's dev default is 12 MCP tools, 13 with clasify; the exact count moves with the build; "≈11.8k extra tokens re-sent on every request" (octocode repo, .octocode/rfc/tool-improvement-2026-10/RFC.md:24, probe results/dedup-20260930, Sep 2026). Click 3, the user's question. Clicks 4 and 5: the search call and its 20 hits with line numbers, small and useful. Clicks 6 and 7: "read the whole file" and its 1,200 lines, mostly noise. It piles onto the stack and squeezes the free space at the top, which is all that's left for the answer. Block heights are illustrative, not measurements (ledger D1). Click 8: the stack is only our way of drawing it. What the model actually receives is one flat stream of tokens, system first, newest last, and it reads all of it from the start on every turn. Click 9: how big that stream may get depends on the model. GPT-4o 128,000 (developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-4o); Claude Opus/Sonnet/Haiku 5.5 1M (platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview); Gemini 3.1 Pro 1,048,576 input tokens (ai.google.dev, gemini-3.1-pro-preview); GPT-6 Astra 1,050,000 (developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-astra); Llama 4 Scout "up to 10M" per Meta, many hosts serve less (dev.meta.ai/llama/models/llama-4); re-checked Oct 2026 (ledger B3). If asked whether bigger is better: NoLiMa (Adobe, arXiv:2502.05167, 7 Feb 2025): "We evaluate 13 popular LLMs that claim to support contexts of at least 128K tokens … At 32K, for instance, 11 models drop below 50% of their strong short-length baselines"; GPT-4o fell "from an almost-perfect baseline of 99.3% to 69.7%" (often misquoted as "10 of 12"). Chroma's LongMemEval run: focused prompts of ~300 tokens scored significantly higher than the full 113k-token input (trychroma.com/research/context-rot, 14 Jul 2025). Claude's own docs: "more context isn't automatically better. As token count grows, accuracy and recall degrade, a phenomenon known as context rot" (platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-windows, 9 Oct 2026) (ledger B4). Extra fact: in Octocode's clasify A/B run, 30 questions delivered 313,115 characters of tool output, yet the Sonnet worker consumed 2.26M input tokens including cache (packages/octocode-benchmark/results/mcp-clasify-ab-111154-2026-10-09.md); that multiplier is the re-sending. Tool-builder lesson: your results stay in the stream for every later turn, so size them like they'll be paid for again and again. Next: how the model actually reads this stream: Q, K and V.
