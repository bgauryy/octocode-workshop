Personal story (speaker's own experience, early 2023; ledger H1). Set the scene (cut from the slide): early 2023, a chat window, no tools, no evidence, and a model eager to agree. Build it click by click down the line: (1) the serious security bug I thought I'd found in Chrome; (2) the model agreeing, and estimating a $10k bounty after ten minutes of chat; (3) the punch: it offered to write the bug-bounty report for me. Click 4: the reversal conversation. I asked it to contradict its own claim, and it folded: "Your intuition is correct: it's not a bug." Pause after it. What moved the model wasn't new evidence, just pressure (one push). Remember that; it explains most of what goes wrong with agents later. Optional, said out loud (not on screen), the cost at scale: Daniel Stenberg ended curl's bug bounty on 31 Jan 2026: "Starting 2025, the confirmed-rate plummeted to below 5%" (from above 15%) amid AI-generated reports (daniel.haxx.se/blog/2026/01/26/the-end-of-the-curl-bug-bounty/); in Jul 2025 he wrote that AI slop was "about 20% of all submissions", each costing "30 minutes, sometimes up to an hour or three" (daniel.haxx.se/blog/2025/07/14/death-by-a-thousand-slops/).
