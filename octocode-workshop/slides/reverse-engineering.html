This is how I learned the tools, and how I still do. Click 1: I reverse-engineered the code: the system prompt a tool ships, every tool description (when to use it, when not), how it fills arguments, how results and errors are phrased back, what it caches, truncates or summarizes. You're reading the decisions of teams who measure everything. Click 2: best practices, meaning the labs' own docs. Example: Claude Code's MCP docs publish its hidden limits: server instructions and each tool description truncated at 2,048 characters, and a 25,000-token cap on tool output (code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp, 9 Oct 2026). Click 3: real examples, so what working tools actually do, not what someone says they do. Click 4: what I didn't learn from: hype and social posts. Click 5: I publish what I find at github.com/bgauryy/open-docs (how AI programs work under the hood, open source), now at 514 stars (GitHub API, 9 Oct 2026; ledger S7).
