Tool descriptions explain one tool at a time. The MCP instructions explain the whole system: how the tools work, how they connect, and the loop that reaches an answer. Octocode MCP was all about context engineering: the server instructions arrive once per session, the tool descriptions and schemas on every request, then the results. Click 1, tool mechanics: each result row carries the fields the next call needs (path, commitSha, line, localPath), and the rule is "Copy row values (path, commitSha, line, localPath); never guess." Click 2, flows between tools: written verbatim in the <github> block: "Unknown repo: ghSearchRepo. Known repo: ghSearchCode | ghStructure. Known path: ghGetFileContent at a commitSha. PR, issue, commit: ghSearchHistory → ghGetHistoryItem." There's a matching <local> route: structureSearch, localSearch, astSearch, then localFetch, lspSearch for identity. Click 3, the research loop, from the <flow> block: "1 zoom out (tree, outline, summary); 2 search narrow (names, text, syntax, history); 3 zoom in on exact lines; 4 prove identity (lspSearch) and behavior, not names or claims; 5 cite." Source: live instructions of the dev build 20.2.0, 12 Oct 2026 (`octocode | jq -r .instructions`), ledger X33. Click 4: together with goal and reasoning on every call and hints in every result, the agent sees the big picture: why it's calling, and where the next hop goes.
