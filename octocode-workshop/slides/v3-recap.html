V3 in one slide: what's new, and what it bought. Each number counts in as the row lands. Agentic CLI: the catalog on a bare pipe, next.* continuations with exit 6, and errors that name the fix; in August the Octocode v18 CLI used 1.99× fewer characters than plain gh (CI 1.52–2.61) at 9.19 vs 9.27 correctness (results/SUMMARY.md:28-30; X8). Rust engine: one native engine behind both surfaces; ~8 ms vs ~120 ms start through the Node launcher, and a warm per-workspace language server takes a TypeScript hover from ~3 s to ~0.1 s (packages/octocode-native/README.md:266-276; CHANGELOG.md:182-184; X3). clasify + Jev: 1.9 KB instead of 644 KB in the docs' example (docs/OCTOCODE_CLASIFY.md:92-95; X6). But honestly: offered in 30 of 30 October sessions and called 0 times (X10). Token savers: deferring 9 rarely-used tools behind one run tool cuts their schema bytes from 10,865 to 1,805 per request, measured 2026-10-09 on the core dist (−83%); plus minimal-by-default rows, matchString windows, contextLines 3 and symbol outlines, as on the previous token slide. Benchmark loop: on 2026-10-09 two laps took Octocode to 0.79× then 0.73× of its own characters on the same 10 questions, correctness 8.4 → 8.6 → 8.5 (results/lean-vs-mcp-114934-2026-10-09.md:388-395, 452-456). And the line at the bottom is the reason I trust the rest: in the same October campaign a disciplined gh with --jq and sed -n used 0.71× of Octocode's characters at tied correctness (X9). Two different ratios, say it out loud: 0.73× is Octocode against its own earlier self (lap 2 vs lap 0); 0.71× is gh-lean against Octocode in the October head-to-head. Moved off the slide: the CLI is the catalog on a bare pipe with next.* continuations and exit 6; the Rust engine is one engine behind MCP and the CLI, and a warm LSP takes a hover from ~3 s to ~0.1 s; clasify + Jev means a judge reads and the agent gets scores + lines; token savers are deferred tools (9 deferred), minimal rows and windows; the benchmark loop has a blind judge and every round is published.
