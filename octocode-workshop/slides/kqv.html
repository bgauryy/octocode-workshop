LLM basics

Each token gets a key, value and query

Like a library: every book has a label and content, and a question finds the right ones.

The algorithm 1 · MatchCompare Q with every K → 2 · WeighBetter match, bigger share → 3 · MixBlend the V's by those shares
Classroom opener: you ask 30 students, "Who has a red backpack?" and look for the ones that match. That's attention, and to do it every token plays three roles. Think of a library. Click 1, K, Key: what I'm about, the label on each book's spine. Click 2, V, Value: what I give, the content inside the book. Click 3, Q, Query: what I need, "I want a book about cats." These are not three algorithms: they are three vectors per token, made by multiplying the token's embedding by three learned matrices (W_Q, W_K, W_V). Click 4, the algorithm in three steps. Match: compare the query with every key (a dot product gives a score). Weigh: turn the scores into shares that add up to 100% (softmax), so better matches get bigger shares. Mix: blend the values by those shares. Engineer's version: a soft dict lookup. Q and K decide where to look; V decides what you take. Source: Vaswani et al. 2017, "Attention Is All You Need", arXiv:1706.03762. Hand-off: next, watch that lookup run on one sentence.
