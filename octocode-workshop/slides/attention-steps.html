1 · Q × KCompare what "it" looks for with each word's label: a match score.
2 · softmaxTurn scores into weights that add up to 100%.
3 · × VBlend each word's content by its weight: "it" now carries "the cat".
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d_k) · V // √d_k just keeps the scores at a manageable size.
Same lookup, on one sentence: "The cat drank milk because it was thirsty." To make sense of the sentence, "it" must know what it refers to. Click 1: "it" builds its Query, roughly: which thing am I? Notice "was" and "thirsty" are greyed out: GPT-style models only look back (the causal mask), so "it" cannot see "thirsty" yet. It has to work it out from the earlier words. Click 2: step 1, Q times K. "it" compares its question with the Key, the label, of every earlier word, and gets a raw match score for each. Click 3: step 2, softmax turns those scores into percentages that add up to 100%, and the bars appear: cat 90, milk 8, drank 2, the rest about zero. These numbers are drawn for this slide, not measured from a real model. Click 4: step 3, times V. "it" blends each word's content, its Value, by those weights, so now "it" carries "the cat". That is the soft dict lookup from the last slide. Click 5: the whole thing in one line, Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d_k) · V, from Vaswani et al. 2017, "Attention Is All You Need", arXiv:1706.03762, Equation 1. For engineers: Q·Kᵀ is a matrix of dot products, one score for every query and key pair. Dividing by √d_k just keeps the scores at a manageable size. Real models run many of these "heads" in parallel, in every layer. Hand-off: notice the K and V of cat, drank and milk never change once computed. So the model keeps them.
