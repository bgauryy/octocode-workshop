One sentence: "The animal didn't cross the street because it was tired." To read the sentence, "it" must know what it refers to. Click 1: "it" builds its Query, roughly: what am I? "was" and "tired" are greyed out: GPT-style models only look back (the causal mask), so "it" cannot see "tired" yet; it has to work it out from the earlier words. Click 2: Compare. "it" takes the dot product of its Query with the Key of every earlier word: one raw match score each. Click 3: Normalize. Softmax turns the scores into weights that add up to 100%, and the bars appear: animal about 80, street 12, cross 4, the rest near zero. These numbers are drawn for this slide, not measured from a real model. Click 4: Mix. "it" takes a weighted sum of each word's Value, so now "it" means the animal. For engineers, the whole thing in one line: Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q·Kᵀ / √d_k) · V, from Vaswani et al. 2017, "Attention Is All You Need", arXiv:1706.03762, Equation 1. Dividing by √d_k keeps the scores from getting too large before softmax. Real models run many heads in parallel in every layer; different heads learn different relations: grammar, meaning, long-distance links. Click 5: the takeaway. The weights always add up to 100%, so related context gets the share and noise dilutes it. Good context means better attention. Anthropic's "Effective context engineering for AI agents" (anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents, 29 Sep 2025) describes this as an "attention budget" that every new token depletes. Hand-off: the K and V of earlier words never change once computed, so the model keeps them.
