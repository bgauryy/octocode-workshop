Why it agrees

Sycophancy

  1. People rate answers that agree with them higher.

  2. RLHF trains on those ratings.Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback: teaches AI to give answers humans prefer, not just predict the next word.

  3. The model learns to please, not to be true.

My $10k bug is documented behaviour. Click 1, sycophancy: Sharma et al., Anthropic, "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models" (arXiv:2310.13548; ledger H3). People prefer answers that match their views, RLHF optimises for those preferences, so the model learns to agree. More receipts. OpenAI's post-mortem (2 May 2025, openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy, quoted via simonwillison.net/2025/May/2/what-we-missed-with-sycophancy/): "an additional reward signal based on user feedback (thumbs-up and thumbs-down data) … weakened the influence of our primary reward signal, which had been holding sycophancy in check"; expert testers said the model "felt" slightly off, but A/B users "liked it". Science (Cheng et al., Stanford, 26 Mar 2026; AP coverage): across 11 AI systems, AI "affirmed users' actions 49% more often than humans" (abstract, doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352; AP coverage words it "than other humans did"). "The very feature that causes harm also drives engagement." The rollback itself: in April 2025 OpenAI rolled back a GPT-4o update for being sycophantic (openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o; ledger H5). That rollback was OpenAI's, not part of the Sharma et al. paper, which is Anthropic's 2023 research. Click 2, FlipFlop: Laban et al., "Are You Sure? Challenging LLMs Leads to Performance Drops in The FlipFlop Experiment" (arXiv:2311.08596; ledger H4). Challenged with "are you sure?", models flip their answer 46% of the time on average, with an average 17% drop in accuracy. The grid is 100 answers, 46 of them flipped. For tool builders this matters: an agent that wants to please will invent a result rather than say "not found". That's why Octocode later demands evidence.
