Open: saving K and V makes a long context cheaper, not sharper. We saw the cost of how many tokens; this slide is about where they sit. Click 1: the curve draws. Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle" (TACL 2024, arXiv:2307.03172; ledger B6): "performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context", and it drops when the fact is in the middle. The curve is the shape of their finding, drawn for the slide, not their numbers (ledger D1). Click 2: the middle band lands. Be precise here: this is a measured position bias. It is not a direct result of the n² cost from the KV cache slide. What it means in practice: in a long chat, your actual task drifts into the middle of the window. Clicks 3–5: so what do you do? These tips are derived advice that follows from those findings, not a measured result. Click 3: one session, one subject. If every earlier message is about the same task, the history the model re-reads is signal, not distraction. Click 4: new task, new session. The old conversation doesn't vanish. It sits in the middle of the window competing for attention. Chroma, Context Rot (14 Jul 2025): "Even a single distractor reduces performance relative to the baseline." Click 5: bring the right context in. Only the files, docs or tool results this task needs. Same idea as Anthropic's guidance to find the smallest set of high-signal tokens. For tool builders: smaller, sharper results beat bigger windows, which is why Octocode returns match windows, ranges, pagination and minimal results.
